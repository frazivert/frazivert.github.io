About

I started at a criminal defense, family law, and personal injury firm in May 2026. The site was already losing traffic when I got there, and my boss and I both knew that going in. So the goal has been steady progress on a big site: figure out which pages matter, improve them, and keep track of what changed and when.

A lot of the job is writing. People searching legal topics are usually stressed and want a straight answer, so I try to write pages that give them one in plain language. That kind of page also tends to be what Google's AI Overviews and tools like ChatGPT end up citing. I send the managing partner an update every week on what moved, what didn't, and what I want to work on next.

I'm finishing an Associate's in Data Science at JCCC, which is where I picked up Python and the habit of checking the numbers before I say something worked.

Case Files

2026-01

Measuring how AI search sees the site

I needed a baseline for AI visibility before I could say whether anything improved it. I set one up in SEMrush and used it to decide which content to work on first.

  • SEMrush AI Visibility
  • Search Console
  • 139K+ AI Overview impressions

SEMrush's AI Visibility tool scored the site 27 out of 100 in August. That's low, but it gave me something to measure against. The more useful number was the breakdown of the prompts the firm could show up for: 44% navigational (people looking for the firm by name), 29% informational (people trying to understand a legal question), and the rest split between commercial, transactional, and task prompts.

Informational prompts are where a well-written answer can get picked up, so the Criminal Defense FAQ pages went to the top of my list (see 2026-02).

I check SEMrush against Search Console because the two don't always agree. Search Console showed the site getting about 1,100 to 1,200 AI Overview impressions a day over the summer, up from around 750 a day when Google started reporting it, and over 139,000 total since May. AI search reporting is still new and pretty noisy, so I treat these numbers as a trend to watch and don't tie them to any single change.

2026-02

Rewriting thin FAQ hub pages

A site audit flagged nine FAQ hub pages for low word count. I wrote real intros for them, then had to wait out a ranking dip that came right after.

  • 9 hubs, then all 15
  • Top 9 pages by impressions
  • "welfare check": 14.8K searches/mo

Each intro explains the topic (arrests, bond, discovery, motions to suppress, and so on) in plain language before the visitor picks a question, and links to the related practice area page. I could have added filler to get past the word count warning, but that wouldn't have helped anyone reading the page.

Right after the intros went live, visibility on those pages fell from about 25% to 13%. My best guess is the new intros were competing with the existing FAQ answers for the same searches. I decided to give it a week or two instead of undoing the changes, and it climbed back. From January through September, the Criminal Defense FAQ pages ended up as the site's top nine pages by impressions, including the page for "welfare check," which gets about 14,800 searches a month.

In October I did the same thing across all 15 Criminal Defense FAQ hubs: new titles, headings, and meta descriptions on all of them, and intros on the seven that didn't have one.

2026-03

18 pages Google dropped from its index

Clicks were down and nobody knew why. I tracked most of the drop to one folder of FAQ pages that Google had stopped indexing, ran a small fix test, and the test didn't work.

  • About 75% of the click drop
  • No penalty
  • Change log by date

Search Console showed that 18 older FAQ pages about police searches fell out of Google's index around August 19 to 20. That accounted for about three quarters of the recent drop in clicks. Before changing anything I checked the things that would have needed a totally different fix: there was no manual action, the robots.txt and sitemap were fine, there were no removal requests, and the live URL tests passed. Google had just decided those pages weren't worth indexing.

For the test, I refreshed three of the pages and their three hub pages with new titles, intros, and internal links, then requested indexing. I logged October 12 as the change date. I keep a change log for every batch of edits (September 29, October 12, and so on) so I can compare each one in Search Console separately.

When I checked on October 26, the three test pages still weren't indexed, so refreshing them one at a time isn't going to be enough. The next step is combining the 18 pages into three or four stronger ones and redirecting the old URLs. Other pages from the same October batches were doing better (see 2026-04).

Overall, average position went from 10.4 to a peak of 6.4. Raw clicks were still down for the year, which has been true for a lot of legal sites since AI Overviews started taking up the top of the results page. I'm not going to claim my work reversed that.

2026-04

Cutting service pages down and fixing the facts

Some of the most important service pages had grown into thousands of words, and some of the legal details were out of date. I rebuilt them and checked the facts against Kansas statutes.

  • Theft defense: 9.1 → 6.7
  • Expungement impressions doubled
  • Checked against K.S.A.

The theft defense page went from 6,000 to 8,000 words down to about 1,800, with the dollar thresholds and penalties corrected. The car accident page went from around 5,000 words to 1,700, with the fault and filing deadline rules fixed. The DUI page was close to 30,000 words. I rebuilt it around what someone with a new DUI charge needs first (penalties, license suspension, the 14-day hearing deadline, diversion) and moved 18 case law summaries into their own blog post. Each page got a clearer title, a short FAQ, and structured data listing an attorney as the author.

About two weeks later, the theft defense page had moved from an average position of 9.1 to 6.7, expungement page impressions had doubled, and a gun rights post I refreshed went from 10 clicks to 17.

While going through older pages I also found problems I didn't think were mine to fix, like a statement on a page with 25,000 impressions that contradicted the case law cited on that same page, and some county phone numbers that may be outdated. I sent each one to the attorneys with the page, the problem, and why it mattered, so they could make the call on the legal content.

2026-05

Nine cities, three practice areas

I wrote and built 27 city pages for Criminal Defense, Family Law, and Personal Injury across nine Johnson County cities, with different copy for each city.

  • 9 cities
  • 3 practice areas
  • LegalService schema

Each page has its own intro, hero section, carousel content, and LegalService structured data. I also fixed menus and formatting that broke on phones. Copy-and-paste city pages are a common problem on local sites, so I wrote each one around that specific city and its courts.

While doing this I found that two sections were made of hand-built pages that weren't actually connected to the CMS. Rebuilding them would have taken a long time for not much payoff, so I recommended leaving them as they are.

2026-06

Structured data I built and then took out

I wrote a Python pipeline that generated FAQ and Article markup for 237 practice area pages. The markup was valid, but it slowed the site down, so I removed it.

  • Python, JavaScript
  • 237 pages
  • Removed for speed

The CMS export flattened the site's page structure, so the script had to rebuild the real URLs before it could generate markup for each page. It also found that about 85% of the FAQ answers in one export were placeholder text, so it rebuilt those from the actual page content.

Getting it onto the live site was harder. The scripts that added the markup to each page weren't reliable on the platform, and while debugging I found two sets of scripts using the same names and deleting each other's output. Even after fixing that, the extra code was slowing pages down, and on a site this size it wasn't worth it. I took it out and wrote up why.

What stayed: BlogPosting markup on 269 blog posts, and zero structured data errors in SEMrush.

2026-07

Fixing review markup with real reviews

The testimonials page had structured data errors because its review markup didn't have ratings. Making ratings up would have cleared the errors and could have gotten the site penalized.

  • 3 → 0 errors
  • Verified Google reviews

Google treats ratings that aren't visible on the page as spam, and that can cost a site its rich results everywhere. I replaced the placeholder testimonials with real Google reviews, which have actual star ratings, and built the markup from those, including an overall rating that matches what visitors see. A SEMrush re-crawl confirmed the errors were gone.

2026-08

A lobby kiosk that goes back to the home screen

The office has a touchscreen directory in the lobby that needed to reset when someone walked away from it. The kiosk software's setting for that didn't work, so I fixed it in the app.

  • React
  • Android, ADB

The app is a single page, so every screen lives at the same URL, and the kiosk's "reload after idle" setting just reloaded whatever screen it was on. I added an idle timer that resets on any touch or key press and goes back home after 20 seconds. I also locked the Android tablet down over ADB so visitors can't leave the app.
